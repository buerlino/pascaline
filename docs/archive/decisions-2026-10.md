### 2026-10-14 ⌫ and Del keep a template whole (F15–F21, R26a–R26h; G2 items 46, 49 and 51 to confirm)
- **Choice:** "⌫ at a bracket" (below) extended to every template the keys build (‸ is the cursor; "box" is a □ of the template). After any ⌫ or Del:
  1. **No half template:** never a half pair (`\left.` with a real `\right)`, or the reverse; the evaluate-at template owns its `\left.`), never an exponent, subscript or limits without its base, never a template that lost a box or a mark (`EQ(,□)`, `\lim_{\to□}`, a matrix cell without its □).
  2. **A box is never deleted:** an emptied box shows □ again, and a selection that is all of a box leaves □ when deleted (R26d). The marks between boxes (the comma of EQ, → of lim, = of Σ and Π, ∂, the `\,` and d of ∫, ° ′ ″, the evaluate-at bar, the `if` of piecewise) and the brackets go only with their whole template. A table's row is never removed: ⌫ at the start of a row steps to the end of the row before it, so a matrix keeps the shape its key gave it (R26g; settled as built by the Opus pass of 2026-10-15; rows become removable only if the owner asks: ⌫ at the start of an empty row's first cell, in a table of more than one row). A body that ⌫ empties character by character (`log 2 ⌫`) shows □, selected, and the next ⌫ removes the template (R23, R26c; settled 2026-10-15).
  3. **⌫ inside a template stays inside it:** at the start of any box but the first, ⌫ steps to the end of the box before it (as ◀) and deletes nothing; Del at the end of any box but the last steps to the next one. An empty exponent on a typed base is the exception: ⌫ at its start removes it alone, the cursor after the base (`5 xʸ ⌫` → `5`; the owner, 2026-10-15). A bracket base still steps, since its pair may be the key's own (the one-line x⁻¹). A stacked fraction is the other exception, from the G2 (item 47): ⌫ at the start of its denominator removes the fraction and joins its parts, the cursor between them (`5/23` → `5‸23`); Del at the end of the numerator is the mirror. A key's argument goes alone the same way: when only the box a #@ key took holds something (a mixed number's whole part, DMS's degrees), ⌫ at the start of any other box removes the template and keeps it. ⌫ right before typed degrees acts on the line (R26h). ⁿ√ at the start of its index selects the root first whenever either box holds something (R26e). ⌫ never jumps out of a box and deletes what stands before the template. lim has no exception: with empty bounds, ⌫ at the start of a typed body steps back through the target and the variable before it removes lim and keeps the body, three presses (R26f; settled as built by the Opus pass of 2026-10-15; an exception like the empty exponent's is added only if it feels slow on the phone).
  4. **⌫ at the start of the first box** removes the template, as "⌫ at a bracket" says: at once when every box is empty; with the content kept when only the main box (`#0` in `spec/keyboard.yaml`) holds something. When another box holds something (a bound, an index, a second cell, a typed exponent), the first ⌫ selects the whole template and a second ⌫ deletes it, so nothing from two boxes is ever joined. This covers a log base, a bound, the evaluate-at bounds (never dropped in one press), an exponent on a pair and EQ's second value (R23; settled as built by the Opus pass of 2026-10-15). Del at the end of the last box is the mirror. The gap between a function's name and its pair (`log‸(`), where ◀ ▶ never stop, counts as the start of the body: ⌫ there acts as above, Del there is point 5's step in (R47, F39). A tap never leaves the cursor in that gap.
  5. **⌫ right after a template** steps into its last box, as it already does after a root or a fraction, and deletes nothing; it never removes a closing bracket alone (F15), and a filled matrix is no longer deleted whole by one ⌫. Del right before a template is the mirror. Built for pairs and the function names that own no box in R26c (`stepIn()` in `app/src/entry/delete.ts`) for powers, a pair's too, in R26d (`deleteAtPower()`), for fractions, roots, ∂ and the mixed number in R26e (`deleteAtBoxes()`), for lim, Σ, Π and ∫ in R26f (`bigOp()`, through `deleteAtBoxes()`), and for matrices, vectors and piecewise in R26g (`table()`, through `deleteAtBoxes()`; no row is removed), and for the log base, EQ, DMS and evaluate-at in R26h (`logBase()`, `equation()`, `dmsAt()`, `evalAt()`, through `deleteAtBoxes()`).
  6. **An exponent stays on its base:** ⌫ never moves an exponent onto another base (`5eˣ` with the e deleted is not `5^…`); when ⌫ empties a base that carries an exponent, the base shows □. Built in R26d (`deleteAtPower()` in `app/src/entry/delete.ts`): eˣ's e and 10ˣ's 10 are the key's own marks and go with their exponent; an exponent 2 or −1 on a □ base counts as the key's own.
  7. **Every way in takes this path:** the ⌫ key, Shift + ⌫ (Del), their auto-repeat, and the hardware Backspace and Delete keys (F16).
  8. **Repeated ⌫ from the end of the line empties it, and so does repeated Del from the start.** R26a's grid test checks points 1–8 on every template.
- **Why:** these are the shapes behind F10 and F15–F21 (Playwright probe over all 32 templates, 2026-10-14): a line that ⌫ cannot finish (24 of 32 templates once a digit is typed in the first box), debris that ⌫ cannot reach (18 of 32 right after inserting), and content moved to where it changes the number (`2 + x² ⌫ ⌫` gave `2²`, `5 eˣ 2 3 ◀ ◀ ⌫ ⌫` gave `5²³`). One rule for every template keeps "one ⌫ undoes one key press" and principle 3. Done through the public API (selection, `insert`, offsets) in `app/src/entry/delete.ts` (R23 creates it), not by patching MathLive.
- **Alternatives:** MathLive's own delete (the bugs); select-then-delete for every template (two presses for every mistaken key); a patch to MathLive's `onDelete` (a fork to maintain).
- **Would change if:** a G2 answer differs. Item 46 asks about ⌫ after a closing bracket (point 5), 49 about a log base digit, and 51 about ⌫ in an empty matrix. The G2 way wins unless it breaks point 1. Also if the owner finds the select step of point 4 slow on the phone.

### 2026-10-13 Definitions survive a relaunch; stored-variable marks; evalf(x,n); root polish (G3)
- **Choice:** (1) stored variables are kept in the defs log as values, not commands: `name:=(value)`, with floats as `evalf(<exact>,k)`, k ≥ 15, so they come back at their precision; a value with free names is replayed first, because Giac does not re-evaluate a stored symbolic value (probe `B:=A*x+1; A:=5; B` → `A*x+1`). **Confirmed by the owner, 2026-10-13** (G3 decision 3). (2) A result that reads stored floats inherits their weakest mark; a perturbation check (each stored float × (1+10^-n)) finds lost digits after cancellation. (3) The agreement check rewrites each `evalf/approx(e,n)` to its own precision; Home and CAS show the n digits asked for, confirmed only (Home: owner, 2026-10-13, G3 decision 2). The float check escalates (P+20 vs P+40) like the exact one. (4) Roots get a second polish at W+20 from `evalf(root,W+20)`, not `exact(root)`: exact() rounds 0.6666666667666… to 2/3 and loses the difference (probe in G3, cube-offset case). (5) The stored defs load times out after 3 s; a store that timed out is never saved over, so a slow IndexedDB cannot wipe the definitions. (6) A value that is 0 in every evaluation but not an exact rational shows ⚠ without a count, never ≈ 0 (owner, 2026-10-13, G3 decision 1; `exp(10^-70)-1`, golden prec-zero-cancel). On the float paths the exact form is unknown, so a float 0 (CAS `evalf(0)`) also shows ⚠.
- **Why:** re-running `romberg` or `rand()` at start is slow or gives a different value; principle 3 (two zeros confirm no digit; `evalf(e,n)` with both runs at n agreed on garbage).
- **Alternatives:** replay the commands as typed (docs/04 6.2 before G3; wrong after reordering, slow, changes `rand()`); for (6), more precision (no precision is enough for an arbitrary cancellation).
- **Would change if:** perturbing every stored float in the same direction misses a cancellation between two of them (`B-C` with B ≈ C both ⚠); then perturb them one at a time. A false ⚠ on a float 0 that users meet often (e.g. a root at 0 from `fsolve`).

### 2026-10-11 Home and CAS share one Giac context, each with its own Ans
- **Choice:** one engine context, so user variables are shared between Home and CAS. Each view keeps its own `Ans` (Giac variables `pasans` for Home, `pcasans` for CAS).
- **Why:** one worker (D2 starts it at launch), no second copy of the 9.8 MB engine in memory. Variables defined in CAS are usable in Home without copying.
- **Alternatives:** two contexts (double the memory and start-up time), or one shared `Ans` (Home decimals would leak into exact CAS work).
- **Known cost (deep-dive review, 2026-10-14):** a value stored in Home is used by CAS without a sign. After Home `3 ▶ x` (the x key types lower-case x in every view), CAS `int(x^2,x)` gives `9*x` and `factor(x^2-1)` gives `8`, and the stored x is replayed after every relaunch (backlog F28). So a CAS result names every stored value it used (packages G20 and R32).
- **Would change if:** the reference shows that Home and CAS keep separate variables (G2 item 52).

### 2026-10-11 CAS time budget
- **Choice:** CAS has a 10 s soft budget (`CAS_SOFT_BUDGET_MS`), after which the UI makes Cancel prominent. Giac's own `timeout 600` is only a safety net. No automatic fallback in CAS (D3).

### 2026-10-11 Cancel replays definitions only
- **Choice:** after Cancel (worker restart, D3), only the user's definitions are replayed, in the form that ran. `Ans` is not replayed, and inputs that use random numbers get new values.
- **Why:** replay must be fast and must not re-run the cancelled job. Definitions are what the user would lose.

### 2026-10-11 Residual check thresholds for Solve and Plot analysis
- **Choice:** a root is accepted silently when its relative residual, evaluated at 30 digits, is ≤ 1e-15. 1-D roots above that are shown with ⚠. For systems, a residual < 1e-6 is shown with ⚠, and anything worse is reported as no solution (Giac's `hybrids` returns the guess unchanged when it fails, so the check is the only guard).
- **Why:** docs/02 section 4 requires substituting every root back. 1e-15 is far above 30-digit noise and far below anything a 12-digit display would show as wrong.
- **Alternatives:** an absolute threshold (fails for large-scale equations), or showing every residual (clutter).
- **Would change if:** golden or user cases show false ⚠ on well-conditioned roots, or the user reverses P5(d) (the reference shows no ⚠; items 12–14, 20).

### 2026-10-12 Powers after a compound term (L8)
- **Choice:** x², xʸ and x⁻¹ (the keys whose template starts with `#@^`) wrap the term before the cursor in brackets when it is a fraction, a root, a power or a mixed number: `1 a/b 2 ▶ x²` → `\left(\frac12\right)^2`, `2 xʸ 3 ▶ x²` → `(2³)²`. After a number, a name, a bracket or a function call the plain `^` stays (`1+3 x²` → `1+3²`, `sin(2) x²` → `sin(2)²`). With the cursor still inside a numerator or denominator the key raises the last term there (`1 ÷ x x²` → `1/x²`); ▶ leaves the part first. Inside a mixed number it raises the whole number.
- **Why:** user, 2026-10-12 (whole fraction after ▶; inside part while inside, so `1/x²` stays easy to type). A bare `^` after a fraction or root gave an empty base or read ambiguously, and after a power MathLive merged the new exponent into the old one (`2^{32}`).
